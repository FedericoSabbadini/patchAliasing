Time-series forecasting is the task of learning the temporal dependencies that link past observations to future values in order to predict them\autocite{wangDeepTimeSeries2026}.

\subsection*{Classic Aliasing}
Whatever the architecture downstream, predictive fidelity is bounded by the digital representation of the signal. The governing limit is the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem: a band-limited continuous-time signal is perfectly reconstructible only if sampled uniformly at a rate $f_s$ strictly greater than twice its highest frequency component $f_{\max}$,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nyquist_criterion}
f_s > 2f_{\max}.
\end{equation}

The threshold $f_N = f_s/2$ is the Nyquist frequency, and when the criterion is violated by an insufficient sampling rate or a missing anti-aliasing filter, components above $f_N$ fold back into the lower spectrum and become indistinguishable from genuine low-frequency content, an irreversible acquisition-time loss known as aliasing, whereas satisfying \autoref{eq:nyquist_criterion} is conventionally treated as sufficient for a discrete sequence to represent its source faithfully.

\subsection*{Evolution of Forecasting Methodologies}
Statistical models such as ARIMA and Exponential Smoothing\autocite[chs.~8--9]{hyndmanForecastingPrinciplesPractice2021} are interpretable but assume linearity and stationarity; deep models relax both, and Transformers buy long-range dependence at a cost quadratic in sequence length\autocite{wangDeepTimeSeries2026}. Patch-based architectures answer that cost by grouping neighbouring observations into tokens. Chronos quantises values into discrete tokens\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}; Chronos-Bolt replaces quantisation with patching and a direct multi-quantile head\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024a,FastAccurateZeroshot2024a,AmazonChronosbolttinyHugging2025}.

Patch-based tokenisation has become the default input stage of time-series foundation models, adopted because it shortens sequences and makes attention affordable. The price is assumed to be resolution: fine detail traded for length, with the sampling theorem deciding what survives, whereas this report argues that the price is of a different kind. Where the period of a signal is commensurate with the patch geometry, consecutive patches repeat, and a frequency the sampler represented perfectly may become poorly observable to the model. Nyquist has nothing to say about such a loss, because it would be caused by the tokeniser rather than by undersampling.

The distinction matters wherever such a model sits in a monitoring or control loop. A blind spot that followed from the configuration rather than from the data would be invisible to an operator who never derived it, stable enough to be relied on by anyone who did, and unreachable by a detector placed downstream of the tokeniser; it would be an engineering defect and a security property at once, and is treated here as a candidate for both.

\subsection*{Application Domain and Agent Objectives}
The reference setting is a designed scenario: a photovoltaic array supplying an internal load, a local storage battery and the external grid, with a control agent performing load balancing, battery dispatch, state monitoring and anomaly detection on short-horizon forecasts. Measured photovoltaic and weather telemetry are taken from the SolarTech Lab dataset\autocite{levaPhotovoltaicPowerWeather2020}; the concepts, states and relations of the scenario are formalised as an OWL2 ontology.

The intended goal for the agent is to base monitoring and power-routing decisions on forecasts produced by a model from the Chronos-Bolt family.

\subsection*{Scope}
The target architecture is Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, with default patch size $P=16$, stride $S=16$, context window $T=2048$, four encoder and four decoder blocks, and a direct quantile projection head\autocite{AmazonChronosbolttinyHugging2025,FastAccurateZeroshot2024a}. The objective is to test the assumption that such a model preserves the frequency content of Nyquist-compliant signals, to characterise how $f_s$, $P$ and $S$ interact in determining when it does not, and to explore how the patch geometry affects the representation of complex inputs, from synthetic signals to photovoltaic telemetry.
