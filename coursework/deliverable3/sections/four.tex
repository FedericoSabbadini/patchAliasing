The candidate set $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ of \nameref{sec:two} is arithmetic and holds for any model, trained or not. Whether a candidate is realised as a loss in a trained network is an empirical proposition, and one that can fail in several ways: the effect may be absent, present but too small to matter, or present in the representation and not in the forecast. Bayesian inference accommodates that structure. Prior assumptions are stated for every estimand before any observation exists, uncertainty is quantified on the natural scale of each effect, and competing formulations, including the mitigation claim, are compared against one another rather than against a null. It also admits a third verdict, that these data do not decide, which for a design of this size is often the honest answer.

\subsection{The hypotheses}

Three claims are carried into the analysis, each stated so that a particular posterior refutes it. A fourth, the mitigation claim $M1$, is stated and tested in \nameref{sec:eight}, since it is the configuration level of Model~A promoted to an estimand rather than a separate model.

\textbf{$H1$, frequency-dependent blind spots.} At a candidate frequency $f_k\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ the model retains less of the signal than at the neighbouring non-candidate frequencies $f_k\pm\delta$. The claim has two readings that need not agree: a behavioural one, in which the forecast rebuilds the tone less faithfully, and a representational one, in which the frequency becomes harder to read out of the internal state. A loss that is absent, or not localised on the candidate set, refutes $H1$.

\textbf{$H2$, independence from temporal alignment.} The magnitude of that loss is a property of the alignment between the signal period and the tokenisation grid, not of the point in its cycle at which the signal is observed. A deficit varying systematically with the starting phase refutes $H2$.

\textbf{$H3$, sites that follow the parameter generating them.} Because $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ is a union, the claim splits in two. $H3a$: at fixed $P$, the sites at $f=c\,f_s/S$ move proportionally to $1/S$. $H3b$: at fixed $S$, the sites at $f=k\,f_s/P$ move proportionally to $1/P$. A site that stays put while the parameter supposed to generate it is varied refutes its branch.

Each claim receives its own measurement, model and estimand. All the models are ordinary regressions built the same way: a measurement, a linear predictor $\mu_i$ carrying one offset per group the observation belongs to, and a likelihood, of which one coefficient is the estimand and the rest keep it unconfounded. \autoref{tab:bayesPriors} gives every prior with its role, and \autoref{sec:appBayes} states each model in full. Every effect prior is centred on no effect, and its scale is varied over the sensitivity ladder $\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$, sceptical, primary and wide, so that a conclusion resting on the choice of scale rather than on the data is detected as such.

\subsection{$H1$}

The behavioural measurement is the forecast amplitude recovery $R=A_{\text{pred}}/A_{\text{true}}$, collected in matched triplets: each candidate $f_k$ with its two controls, all three sharing the same background realisation and the same phase, which is what makes the contrast paired. The contrast is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2contrast}
d = \log(R_k + 0.01) - \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\log(R_- + 0.01) + \log(R_+ + 0.01)\right],
\end{equation}

one number per triplet, negative when the candidate recovers less than its neighbours. Model~A fits it hierarchically,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2hier}
d_i \sim \text{Student-}t_4(\mu_i,\ \sigma),
\quad
\mu_i = \beta_{c[i]} + u_{k[i]} + u_{b[i]},
\quad
\beta_c \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar\beta + \delta_O \widetilde O_c + \delta_P \widetilde{\log P}_c,\ \tau\right),
\end{equation}

with one offset per configuration, per lock harmonic and per background draw. The estimand is the population effect $\bar\beta$, read as the recovery ratio $e^{\bar\beta}$. To verify: $e^{\bar\beta}<1$ is the attenuation $H1$ predicts, whereas a posterior at or above $1$ says that candidate frequencies are not attenuated in the forecast and the behavioural half of $H1$ fails. The configuration-level slope $\delta_O$ of the same fit carries $M1$, and is read in \nameref{sec:eight}.

Model~B poses the same question of the representation. At each candidate frequency $f_c$ a probe separates tones at $f_c\pm1$\,Hz from a captured internal state, and the response $L_i$ is the prequential codelength of those labels, in bits. The regression is Gamma with a log link,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2mdl}
L_i \sim \text{Gamma}\!\left(k,\ k/\mu_i\right),
\qquad
\log \mu_i = \alpha_0 + \theta_{lock}\,\mathrm{IsLocked}_i + s_{\text{st}[i]} + g_{\text{geo}[i]},
\end{equation}

so that $e^{\theta_{lock}}$ is the factor by which the codelength of a candidate frequency expands, with probe stage and geometry entering as zero-mean offsets. To verify: $e^{\theta_{lock}}>1$ is the representational cost $H1$ predicts, while $\theta_{lock}$ near zero says the frequency remains as readable at a candidate as anywhere else.

\subsection{$H2$}

$H2$ uses the same triplets with one change. \autoref{eq:d2contrast} has already averaged over phase, so the phase index is kept instead and the response becomes the per-phase deficit $y_i = \log(R_{\text{ctrl},i}+0.01)-\log(R_{\text{lock},i}+0.01)$, positive when the candidate is worse. Model~C is Model~A on that response, without the configuration-level covariates and with the phase circle cut into eight slices, each given its own offset $u_p$:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2phase}
y_i \sim \text{Student-}t_4(\mu_i,\ \sigma),
\qquad
\mu_i = \beta_{c[i]} + u_{k[i]} + u_{b[i]} + u_{p[i]},
\qquad
u_p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\phi).
\end{equation}

The estimand $\sigma_\phi$ is the spread of those eight offsets. To verify: $\sigma_\phi$ near zero means the offsets are indistinguishable and the deficit is set by the geometry alone, as $H2$ predicts, whereas a large $\sigma_\phi$ means it depends on where in its cycle the signal starts. Dropping $u_p$ gives a nested null, compared with the full model by leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO).

\subsection{$H3$}

Both branches are tested on the token collapse $z_g(f)$, the dispersion of the input-patch embeddings across the patches of one context, which is zero when consecutive patches are identical. All geometries share one sweep grid, so that none is scored only where its own theory predicts a dip.

The first question is where the dips are located. Each swept frequency carries two labels, one per branch:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2sites}
\log z_g(f) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(
\alpha_g + \theta_S \cdot \text{OnStrideGrid} + \theta_P \cdot \text{OnPatchGrid},\ \sigma
\right),
\end{equation}

with $\alpha_g$ the per-geometry off-grid level, and the label coefficients $\theta_S$ and $\theta_P$ as estimands. To verify: $H3$ predicts both negative, and the two-label fit should win the LOO comparison against each label alone and against neither; coefficients near zero mean the dips bear no relation to either grid.

The second question is whether the dips move, since \autoref{eq:d2sites} remains compatible with dips that merely sit on a fixed grid. Every detected dip is assigned to the branch that predicts it, sites belonging to both are set aside, and the fundamental $\hat f^{\,F}_{1,g}$ of branch $F$ in geometry $g$ is compared with the spacing that branch predicts, $\Delta^S_g=f_s/S_g$ or $\Delta^P_g=f_s/P_g$:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2move}
\hat f^{\,F}_{1,g} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(
\kappa_F \,\Delta^F_g,\ \sqrt{\sigma_F^2+\Delta f^2}
\right).
\end{equation}

The slope $\kappa_F$ is the estimand, measured spacing per unit of predicted spacing. To verify: $\kappa_F=1$ denotes a branch that tracks its own arithmetic, and any other value refutes that branch. Identification here is a property of the design and not of the model, since $\kappa_S$ requires a series holding $P$ fixed while $S$ varies and $\kappa_P$ the converse; \autoref{tab:hfModels} supplies both. The grid, the signals and the order in which the analysis executes are set out in \nameref{sec:five}.
