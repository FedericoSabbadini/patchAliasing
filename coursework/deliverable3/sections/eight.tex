\subsection{The proposed mitigation}

One mitigation is carried through the design and pre-registered as $M1$: increase the patch overlap, that is shorten the stride $S$ at fixed patch size $P$, which raises the overlap ratio $O$ of \autoref{eq:overlap}. Its mechanism is arithmetic. The stride branch places its lowest in-band member at $f_s/S$, so halving the stride doubles that frequency and removes every member of the family that fell below it, thinning what remains below Nyquist. The claim under test is that this reduces the deficit; a deficit that does not shrink, or that grows, with overlap refutes $M1$. The test is Model~A$'$ of \nameref{sec:four}, which is Model~A read at its configuration level: the estimand is the overlap slope $\delta_O$, and $M1$ is supported only if $\delta_O<0$ with posterior probability at least $0.95$.

\subsection{What the test returned}

The posterior of $\delta_O$ is $0.44\;[-0.03,\,0.81]$ on the clean set and $0.38\;[0.01,\,0.72]$ on the full one. Both are positive: higher overlap is associated with a larger contrast, not a smaller one. The probability that $\delta_O<0$ is $0.036$ and $0.023$, far below the threshold, so the verdict is \textbf{refuted} rather than inconclusive.

Two readings are consistent with that result. Shortening $S$ at fixed context multiplies the token count by $P/S$, so the geometry effect and the effect of a longer token sequence cannot be separated within this design. Independently of that confound, overlap thins only the stride branch and leaves the patch branch untouched, so the relative weight of the patch component grows as $S$ falls and the aggregate contrast can move in the unexpected direction.

The arithmetic bounds what overlap could have achieved even had the test come out the other way. The stride fundamental $f_s/S$ rises without limit as $S$ falls, but the patch fundamental $f_s/P$ depends on $P$ alone, so every integer multiple of it remains a candidate at any stride. Expressed in the dimensionless terms of \nameref{sec:two}, overlap moves the cycles-per-stride comb and leaves the cycles-per-patch comb fixed. On the one-minute photovoltaic telemetry of the application domain, where $f_s=1/60$\,Hz and $P=16$, the patch harmonics have periods of $16/k$ minutes and stay blind under any stride reduction whatever. The firmest practical conclusion of this report is therefore negative: an operator who raises the overlap ratio cannot record the risk as mitigated.

\subsection{Consequences for the control agent}

That conclusion determines what the agent of \autoref{sec:appendixA} can and cannot do. The ontology makes the tokenisation geometry a first-class object rather than a property hidden inside the model, so the candidate set is derivable from $(f_s,P,S)$ by arithmetic on the knowledge base, and an \pv{ObservabilityAssessment} can mark a frequency as a candidate the moment a configuration is declared. Detection is therefore cheap and requires no experiment.

Acting on it is the harder half. The agent forecasts with Chronos, and the only mitigation available inside that model has just been refuted, so it cannot repair the forecast it depends on. What remains is honest abstention: the agent raises an alert and degrades its own authority rather than continuing to act on a signal it cannot vouch for. A confirmed \valueof{StructurallyAliased} assessment forces the safe mode and suspends forecast-driven battery dispatch; a merely \valueof{PartiallyObservable} one reduces the battery cap and lowers decision confidence. The value the agent adds here is not correction but the refusal to propagate an unwarranted forecast into an actuator.

\subsection{Representing, monitoring, explaining and acting}

Four capabilities follow, and each is supported by one of the two analyses this report carries. \textbf{Representation} is semantic: patch size, stride and the derived overlap ratio are properties of a \pv{PatchedRepresentation}, and one signal may hold several, one per geometry under evaluation, so the risk is attached to a configuration rather than asserted of a model. \textbf{Monitoring} is arithmetic on that representation: because $H3a$ confirms that the sites track $f_s/S$ one for one, the candidate set can be recomputed whenever a sampling interval, a patch size or a checkpoint changes, without re-measurement. Detection of content at those frequencies belongs on the raw telemetry, upstream of tokenisation, since anything placed after it inherits the blind spot it is meant to guard.

\textbf{Explanation} is where the Bayesian layer does work that a threshold could not. Each assessment records the estimand, its effect size, its credible interval and its posterior probability, so the reason a frequency is flagged is auditable by a human operator and expressed on a scale that operator can interpret, rather than delivered as an opaque score. The three-way verdict is preserved in the semantics: \valueof{PartiallyObservable} is the ontology's rendering of \emph{inconclusive}, so an unfinished or undecided test is visible as such instead of being resolved by assumption in either direction. \textbf{Action} then follows from the assessment through mode selection, which is the point at which uncertainty about a learned representation reaches an actuator in a bounded and reversible form.

\subsection{A direction for future work}

Since the deficit cannot be removed inside a single patch geometry, the natural next candidate is not to repair the forecaster but to route around it. The nulls of a patch tokeniser are determined by its own $(f_s,P,S)$, so two models with different geometries are blind at different frequencies, and a third with no patch grid at all, a classical spectral or state-space forecaster, is blind at neither. An ensemble in which the agent selects the forecaster per frequency, using the observability assessment it already computes as the switch, would leave no frequency served only by a model known to be blind there. The semantic layer needed to arbitrate such a choice is the one described above, so the addition is a second forecaster rather than a second architecture for the agent. Its cost is that the two forecasters must be calibrated against each other, and the claim remains untested here: establishing it would require the same paired contrast of \nameref{sec:four} applied to the ensemble output rather than to a single model.
