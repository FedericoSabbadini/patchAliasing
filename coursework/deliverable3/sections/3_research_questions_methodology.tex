\subsection{Expected Behaviour at Blind-Spot Frequencies}\label{sec:three}
\begingroup
\let\subsubsection\paragraph
\let\subsection\subsubsection
Bayesian statistics represents uncertainty about unknown quantities through probability distributions and updates prior beliefs when data are observed\autocite{murphyProbabilisticMachineLearning2023}. It is used here because the hypotheses concern the magnitude and uncertainty of structural aliasing effects: posterior distributions quantify evidence for a localisation loss, phase independence and parameter-tracking against predeclared decision thresholds. The candidate set $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ of \autoref{eq:flock} is arithmetic and holds for any model. Three claims are carried into the analysis, each stated so that a particular posterior refutes it.

\autoref{tab:bayesModels} pairs each claim with the model that decides it, its estimand, the value the claim predicts and the measurements it is fitted to, and \autoref{sec:appBayes} states each model in full. Every effect prior is centred on no effect, and its scale is varied over the sensitivity ladder $\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$, sceptical, primary and wide, so that a conclusion resting on the choice of scale rather than on the data is detected as such.

\subsubsection*{Frequency-dependent blind spots (H1)}
At a candidate frequency $f_k\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ the model retains less of the signal than at the two neighbouring control frequencies $f_k\pm\delta$, which are not themselves candidates. The claim has two readings that need not agree: a behavioural one, in which the forecast no longer rebuilds the tone at the frequency it was given, and a representational one, in which the frequency becomes harder to read out of the internal state. A loss that is absent, or not localised on the candidate set, refutes $H1$. Phase-locking is treated here as a necessary but not sufficient condition for the specific geometry-induced structural aliasing this study hypothesises: nothing in the design excludes that distinct signals acquire near-identical learned representations for reasons unrelated to $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$, and no claim is made about frequencies outside it.

\subsubsection*{Independence from temporal alignment (H2)}
The magnitude of that loss is a property of the alignment between the signal period and the tokenisation grid, not of the point in its cycle at which the signal is observed. A loss varying systematically with the starting phase refutes $H2$.

\subsubsection*{Parametric dependence of blind spot sites (H3)}
Because $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ is a union, the claim splits in two, one per branch, with $c,k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}$ the harmonic indices of \autoref{eq:branches}. $H3a$: at fixed $P$, the sites at $f=c\,f_s/S$ move proportionally to $1/S$. $H3b$: at fixed $S$, the sites at $f=k\,f_s/P$ move proportionally to $1/P$. A site that stays put while the parameter supposed to generate it is varied refutes its branch.

\subsection{The models}

Five models carry the claims and \autoref{tab:bayesModels} pairs each with the one it decides. \autoref{tab:bayesPriors} gives every parameter below with its prior and its role. Three responses serve them: a paired behavioural contrast on the forecast, a codelength on a captured internal state, and the dispersion of the input-patch embeddings. Each is stated in the form it is fitted, with the reasoning behind every term left to \autoref{sec:appBayes}.

\subsubsection*{Model A, the behavioural reading (H1)}
The measurement is the forecast amplitude recovery $R=A_{\mathrm{pred}}/A_{\mathrm{true}}$, collected in matched triplets: a candidate $f_k$ with its two controls $f_k\pm\delta$, all three sharing one background realisation and one phase, so that only the frequency changes. The contrast is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2contrast}
d = \log(R_k + 0.01) - \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\log(R_- + 0.01) + \log(R_+ + 0.01)\right],
\end{equation}

one number per triplet, negative when the candidate recovers less than its neighbours, and formable only because the injected tone has a known frequency. Model~A fits it as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2hier}
d_i \sim \text{Student-}t_4(\mu_i,\ \sigma),
\quad
\mu_i = \beta_{c[i]} + u_{k[i]} + u_{b[i]},
\quad
\beta_c \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar\beta + \delta_O \widetilde O_c + \delta_P \widetilde{\log P}_c,\ \tau\right).
\end{equation}

The response is already a paired difference, so the effect is the intercept rather than a term beside it: $e^{\bar\beta}$ is recovery at a candidate relative to its controls and $H1$ predicts $e^{\bar\beta}<1$. The heavy tail absorbs the arms that rebuild nothing, where the logarithm returns very large contrasts and a Gaussian would let a handful of them set the estimate; the three offsets keep an unusual harmonic or a single generator from being read as the effect.

\subsubsection*{Model A$'$, the mitigation claim (M1)}
Not a separate fit but the configuration level of \autoref{eq:d2hier} promoted to an estimand,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2mitigation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\beta_c\right] = \bar\beta + \delta_O\,\widetilde O_c + \delta_P\,\widetilde{\log P}_c,
\qquad
\widetilde O_c=\frac{O_c-\bar O}{0.5},
\qquad
\widetilde{\log P}_c=\log P_c-\overline{\log P},
\end{equation}

the tilde denoting centring on the design, which leaves $\bar\beta$ the effect at the average configuration rather than an extrapolation to $O=0$ and $P=1$. The two covariates are estimable together because \autoref{tab:hfModels} varies overlap and patch size independently. A mitigation raises $d$ towards zero, so $\delta_O>0$ is the direction $M1$ predicts and a slope at or below zero refutes it.

\subsubsection*{Model B, the representational reading (H1)}
Model~B reads the same claim off the representation by minimum description length (MDL) probing\autocite{voitaInformationTheoreticProbingMinimum2020}: at each candidate $f_c$ a linear probe is asked to tell a tone at $f_c-1$\,Hz from one at $f_c+1$\,Hz using a captured internal state alone, over the ten probe stages named in \autoref{sec:appBayes}, and the response $L_i$ is the prequential codelength of that sequence of labels in bits. A codelength is positive and right-skewed with a variance growing in its mean, so the regression is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2mdl}
L_i \sim \text{Gamma}\!\left(r,\ r/\mu_i\right),
\qquad
\log \mu_i = \alpha_0 + \theta_{\mathrm{lock}}\,\mathrm{IsLocked}_i + s_{\text{st}[i]} + g_{\text{geo}[i]},
\end{equation}

with probe stage and geometry entering as zero-mean offsets because codelengths differ several-fold between them. The log link of \autoref{eq:d2mdl} makes the estimand multiplicative: $e^{\theta_{\mathrm{lock}}}>1$ is the representational cost $H1$ predicts, and $\theta_{\mathrm{lock}}$ near zero says a candidate frequency is as readable as anywhere else.

\subsubsection*{Model C, temporal alignment (H2)}
Every triplet is injected at one starting phase $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ of the tone, and the sweep is repeated at several of them, so the same candidate is observed at several points of its own cycle. Model~A carries no phase term: its triplets are pooled across $\phi$, which leaves any phase dependence in the residual where it cannot be inspected. $H2$ is a claim about exactly that dependence, so Model~C is Model~A with the phase index retained as a grouping and the configuration-level covariates dropped, those being about the design axes rather than about alignment. The circle $[0,2\pi)$ is cut into eight equal slices, each given its own offset, and the response is the per-phase deficit $y_i=-d_i$, positive when the candidate is worse:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2phase}
y_i \sim \text{Student-}t_4(\mu_i,\ \sigma),
\qquad
\mu_i = \beta_{c[i]} + u_{k[i]} + u_{b[i]} + u_{p[i]},
\qquad
u_p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\phi).
\end{equation}

The estimand $\sigma_\phi$ of \autoref{eq:d2phase} is the spread of those eight offsets, near zero when the geometry alone sets the deficit, as $H2$ predicts, and large when it depends on where in its cycle the signal starts. Slices presuppose no shape for a dependence, and dropping $u_p$ gives the nested null the leave-one-out comparison is made against. One limit belongs here rather than with the results: Model~C reads the spread of the same response Model~A reads, so where the population effect is itself indistinguishable from zero little remains for phase to move, and $H2$ is read conditionally.

\subsubsection*{Model D1, where the sites are (H3)}
Both branches are read on the token collapse $z_g(f)$, the dispersion in geometry $g$ and at frequency $f$ of the input-patch embeddings across the patches of one context, which is zero when consecutive patches are identical. Every swept frequency carries two binary labels recording whether it lies on the stride grid $\mathcal{F}_{S}$ or the patch grid $\mathcal{F}_{P}$:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2sites}
\log z_g(f) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(
\alpha_g + \theta_S \cdot \text{OnStrideGrid} + \theta_P \cdot \text{OnPatchGrid},\ \sigma
\right),
\end{equation}

with $\alpha_g$ the per-geometry off-grid level and the label coefficients as estimands. $H3$ predicts both negative and the two-label fit winning the leave-one-out comparison against each one-label alternative.

\subsubsection*{Model D2, whether the sites move (H3a, H3b)}
\autoref{eq:d2sites} remains compatible with dips sitting on a grid fixed once and for all, so the measured spacing is regressed on the spacing each branch predicts:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d2spacing}
\hat f^{\,F}_{1,g} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(
\kappa_F \,\Delta^F_g,\ \sqrt{\sigma_F^2+\Delta f^2}
\right).
\end{equation}

The slope $\kappa_F$ of \autoref{eq:d2spacing} is measured spacing per unit of predicted spacing, so $\kappa_F=1$ is the claim, and the residual scatter carries the floor $\Delta f$, the sweep step, because a spacing read off a grid cannot be sharper than the grid. Identification is not symmetric between the branches and the condition belongs before the fits: a site informs $\kappa_S$ only on $\mathcal F_S \setminus \mathcal F_P$ and $\kappa_P$ only on $\mathcal F_P \setminus \mathcal F_S$, and a branch reaching fewer than ten unambiguous sites is reported as not identified rather than as a null.



\subsection{Decisions and validation}

Every threshold in \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions} is fixed before a posterior is inspected, and validation comes before interpretation. Beside each rule the table records the probability that the rule already scored under the prior, so that a posterior counts as evidence only in so far as it has moved away from that value. A claim is supported at $0.95$, refuted at $0.05$, and otherwise reported as inconclusive, which is a verdict in its own right.

Interpretation is gated by three preregistered convergence diagnostics, and competing formulations are compared by leave-one-out cross-validation under a reading rule that returns an inconclusive verdict for differences within twice their standard error. The diagnostics, the reading rule and the sampler settings shared by every fit are stated in \autoref{sec:appBayes}; a fit failing any diagnostic is not used to support a claim.

\subsection{Signals and Data}\label{sec:four}
\begingroup
\let\subsection\subsubsection
Four kinds of signal appear in this study. Three are synthetic and are what the models are probed with, each serving a different purpose rather than being a variant of the others; the fourth is the real telemetry of the application domain. This section states what each source is and why it is in the study; the appendices carry the pseudocode, the parameters and the pool composition.

\subsection*{Single-harmonic sinusoid}

It is the primitive the design rests on: frequency and phase are exact, so a locked frequency produces exactly the degeneracy of \autoref{eq:token_collapse} and no background content can be blamed for a loss observed at a predicted site. It takes the form $x(t)=A\sin(2\pi f t+\phi)$, sampled at $f_s$.

\subsection*{Light TSMixup}

A controlled probing variant of the mixup augmentation used to train Chronos\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}, in which the real source datasets of the original are replaced by a pool of sinusoids. It tests whether the effect survives when the tone is embedded in a multi-component signal whose composition is still fully known. Its pseudocode and parameters are given in \autoref{sec:appB}.

\subsection*{KernelSynth}

The Gaussian-process generator of the same training recipe\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}. Its output carries trends and broadband content as well as periodicity, so it tests whether the effect survives in signals resembling the corpus the model was trained on rather than the probe used to measure it. Its pseudocode, kernel bank and parameters are given in \autoref{sec:appC}.

\subsection*{Photovoltaic telemetry}

The measured series of the SolarTech Lab at Politecnico di Milano, released open access on IEEE DataPort\autocite{levaPhotovoltaicPowerWeather2020}. Six channels are recorded at one-minute resolution and timestamped in Central European Time: module power at a $30^\circ$ tilt, ambient temperature, global horizontal irradiance, global irradiance in the plane of array, wind speed and wind direction. Missing samples are frequent and structural. They supply the quantities the ontology of \autoref{sec:appendixA} names and the cadence against which the dimensionless readings of \autoref{sec:tokenisation} are interpreted. The set is the application domain of this study, no hypothesis is tested on it, \autoref{sec:appSolar} reports how it is treated and what is read off it.
\endgroup

\subsection{Experimental Framework}\label{sec:five}
\begingroup
\let\subsubsection\paragraph
\let\subsection\subsubsection
Testing the hypotheses of \autoref{sec:three} requires a design in which the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ can be told apart, signals whose frequency content is known by construction, and an order of execution in which nothing that decides a hypothesis is chosen after the data have been seen.

Three things are deliberately outside this design. Variation between independently trained instances is not measured, since one model is trained per geometry. The photovoltaic series supplies the application domain and the semantic layer, not an experimental corpus: no hypothesis is tested on measured telemetry. And no untrained network is probed, so the claim that token collapse precedes learning rests on the arithmetic rather than on a baseline.

The fifteen geometries this analysis is carried out on, and the reason they had to be retrained, are given in \autoref{sec:geometries}; \autoref{tab:hfModels} lists them. That the two parameters vary independently there is what lets Models~A, D1 and~D2 separate the branches and estimate the overlap and patch-size covariates without confounding them.

\subsection{Analysis design}

Synthetic signals are sampled at $f_s=512$\,Hz and analysed over $[2,250]$\,Hz, strictly inside the Nyquist limit, whereas the telemetry is recorded once per minute, so absolute frequencies are not comparable in hertz. The transferable quantity is dimensionless: a candidate completes a whole number of cycles within one stride or one patch, so $\mathrm{cpp}=k$ corresponds, for a one-minute series with $P=16$, to a period of $16/k$ minutes. A single seed controls all stochastic generation, and one model is trained per geometry, so instance-to-instance variation is never measured and a small difference between configurations cannot be ascribed to geometry alone.

\subsubsection{Exploratory frequency sweep}

Pure tones cross the band in one-hertz steps at several phases, on contexts near $512$ samples shortened to a whole number of strides. The behavioural reading appends the median forecast recursively to $512$ generated samples and takes the largest non-zero Fourier component as the reconstructed frequency, recording what the model produces. The representational reading trains a linear probe on a reduced subspace of the output quantile head and asks whether the frequency stays linearly readable before that output is formed. It is scored on the seven hierarchical band-classification tasks of \autoref{tab:bandTasks}, after Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}, whose three levels of binary split place a tone in one of eight buckets, and on a local task of constant difficulty everywhere separating $f-1$\,Hz from $f+1$\,Hz. \autoref{sec:appSweep} states how each is scored and \autoref{fig:rec16-16} shows both. The scan is on the integer grid and evaluates no lock condition exactly: where a geometry has non-integer candidates it resolves their neighbourhood rather than the site. Averaging over phase and carrying no controls, its purpose is not to test a hypothesis but to establish whether the model represents frequency content at all and where that ability degrades. The inferential stage below places every candidate and control at its exact value instead.

The truncation cancels within a triplet, which compares a candidate with its own controls under one geometry, but not between geometries; \autoref{sec:appBayes} states what it does and does not confound, and it is reported rather than adjusted for.

The design is fixed before anything is measured: the configurations, the frequency grid and the coverage each geometry must reach are declared once, and a session collecting only part of it records that as a limitation rather than a new design. Each background is archived with its recipe and a content hash and admitted only if both still agree, and the collected rows are checked against the declared design, for unambiguous sites per branch and usable rows per geometry, before any model is fitted.

No response filter is applied at that point. Under \autoref{eq:d2contrast} an arm in which the forecast rebuilds no measurable tone returns a large negative contrast rather than an undefined one, so those rows are retained rather than discarded and the design keeps the cases where the loss it looks for should be largest.

\autoref{tab:bayesModels} pairs each claim with the model that decides it and \autoref{sec:appBayes} states the likelihoods, priors and sampler settings. Model~A pools the recovery triplets across geometries and carries overlap and log patch size at its configuration level, so $M1$ is a reading of the same fit rather than a separate one. Models~B, C and~D1 each have a nested counterpart, unadjusted, phase-free and single-grid, compared by leave-one-out cross-validation read with its uncertainty rather than by rank. Model~D2 assigns each detection to one branch and sets aside the sites both grids predict. Both D1 and D2 are fitted on token dispersion, which is a property of the tokenisation: a collapse of consecutive token embeddings at $f=c f_s/S$ follows from the arithmetic and would appear in an untrained network. They therefore locate candidate sites, and the evidence that a trained model loses information there is carried by Models~A and~B, which are measured on the forecast and on the representation against matched controls.

Interpretation is fail-closed. Convergence, parameter recovery for every likelihood family, posterior predictive adequacy pooled and stratified by geometry and generator, prior and likelihood sensitivity, and the reliability of the leave-one-out estimates are explicit gates, fixed with the thresholds of \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions}. A gate that fails does not weaken a claim: the estimand is reported as not reportable, and a branch with fewer than ten unambiguous sites as not identified, neither of which is a null result. Every estimand is returned to its natural scale and reported by its posterior median and $95\%$ credible interval, with the probability of its threshold and, where defined, its region of practical equivalence.
