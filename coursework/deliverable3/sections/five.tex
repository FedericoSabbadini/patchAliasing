Testing the hypotheses of \nameref{sec:four} requires a design in which the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ can be told apart, signals whose frequency content is known by construction, and an order of execution in which nothing that decides a hypothesis is chosen after the data have been seen. This section fixes all three.

\subsection{The grid of geometries}

Since any change to $P$ or $S$ requires retraining, the design is a grid of variants trained from scratch rather than a set of published checkpoints, each described by its patch size, its stride and the overlap ratio $O$ of \autoref{eq:overlap}. Configurations with $S>P$ are excluded by construction: they leave $S-P$ unobserved samples between consecutive patches, so their loss is one of discarded samples and not of tokenisation geometry, as \nameref{sec:two} sets out.

\input{tables/hfModels.tex}

\autoref{tab:hfModels} lists the retrained runs, and two properties of the grid do the identifying work. First, $P$ and $S$ vary independently: series at fixed $P$ with varying $S$ ($P\in\{16,24,32\}$) isolate the stride, while series at fixed $S$ with varying $P$ ($S\in\{8,12,16\}$) isolate the patch. This is what lets Models~A and~D separate the two branches and estimate the overlap and patch-size covariates without confounding them. Second, the divisibility columns decide whether a geometry can attribute anything at all: where $S \mid P$ every stride site is also a patch site and no stride-only frequency exists, and at $P=S$ the two combs coincide entirely. Six of the fifteen runs have $S \nmid P$, and those are the ones on which $\theta_S$ and $\kappa_S$ rest. All share the same training budget and seed, so a difference between them is a difference of geometry and not of training.

\subsection{Signal generators}

Three generators supply the inputs, each for a different purpose rather than as a variant of the others.

\textbf{Single-harmonic sinusoid}, $x(t)=A\sin(2\pi f t+\phi)$, sampled at $f_s$. It is the primitive the design rests on: frequency and phase are exact, so a locked frequency produces the degeneracy of \nameref{sec:two} exactly and no background content can be blamed for a loss observed at a predicted site.

\textbf{Light TSMixup}, a controlled probing variant of the mixup augmentation used to train Chronos\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}, which replaces the real source datasets of the original with a pool of sinusoids and returns a Dirichlet-weighted convex combination of mean-scaled draws from it. It tests whether the effect survives when the tone is embedded in a multi-component signal whose composition is still fully known. Its pseudocode and parameters are given in \autoref{sec:appB}.

\textbf{KernelSynth}, the Gaussian-process generator of the same training recipe, which composes kernels drawn from a fixed bank under sum and product operators and samples from the resulting prior. Its output carries trends and broadband content as well as periodicity, so it tests whether the effect survives in signals resembling the corpus the model was trained on rather than the probe used to measure it. Its pseudocode, kernel bank and parameters are given in \autoref{sec:appC}.

Neither generated family is merely an input: each background realisation enters Models~A and~C as a grouping level, so a disagreement between the two would be absorbed by the scale $\sigma_b$ and made visible rather than averaged into the residual.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}

All signals are sampled at $f_s=512$\,Hz and analysed over $[2,250]$\,Hz, strictly inside the Nyquist limit $f_s/2=256$\,Hz, so every tone probed is fully representable under the Nyquist--Shannon theorem and a loss observed at a predicted site cannot be attributed to undersampling. A single seed controls all stochastic generation.

The complete experimental grid is built before any data are collected: one row per combination of geometry, candidate frequency, background type and phase. Where a tone rides on a background it does so at a fixed signal-to-noise ratio of $4$ over a unit-variance realisation, so the tone whose fate is tracked is always identifiable. Each candidate frequency is paired with two controls placed as far from it as the design allows without either control landing on another member of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$; a site for which no such offset exists is dropped rather than compared against a contaminated control. Phases are drawn from the non-redundant set available at each frequency, since a tone at $f$ admits only finitely many distinct alignments with the sample grid.

The Bayesian fits share a fixed $480$-sample probing context, which has to close on a whole number of strides: otherwise the final patch is internally padded and the padding rather than the geometry would produce a collapse. A geometry whose stride does not divide the context is therefore swept descriptively but not fitted.

\subsection{Decisions and validation}

Every threshold in \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions} is fixed before a posterior is inspected, and validation comes before interpretation. The table records beside each rule the probability that rule already scored under the prior, so that a posterior counts as evidence only in so far as it has moved away from that value. A claim is supported at $0.95$, refuted at $0.05$, and otherwise reported as inconclusive, which is a verdict in its own right.

Interpretation is gated by three preregistered convergence diagnostics, and comparison between competing formulations by leave-one-out cross-validation under a reading rule that returns \emph{inconclusive} for differences within twice their standard error. Both, together with the sampler settings shared by every fit, are stated in \autoref{sec:appBayes}; a fit failing any diagnostic is not used to support a claim.

\subsection{Experimental workflow}

The analysis executes in a fixed order, and the order is itself part of the design: every quantity that decides a hypothesis is specified before the model that could be tuned to it is loaded. Each stage writes its output to disk before the next begins, so that an interrupted run resumes without any statistical choice being revisited.

\subsubsection{Exploratory frequency sweep}

The first stage is descriptive and uses no Bayesian machinery. Pure sinusoids are swept across the band in one-hertz steps, and two measurements are taken of every tone in every geometry: the dominant frequency of an autoregressive rollout, which records what the model outputs, and the cross-validated accuracy of a linear probe on the projected quantile head, which records what its internal state retains. The two may disagree, and a disagreement is diagnostic, since a frequency that is no longer reconstructed but remains readable implicates the projection head rather than the tokenisation. It decides nothing on its own, since it averages over phase, uses a single signal family and reports no uncertainty. Both readings are given in \autoref{sec:appSweep} and summarised in \nameref{sec:seven}.

\subsubsection{Design and priors}

Priors are fixed before any observation exists. The skeleton of the experiment, one planned row per geometry, candidate frequency, background realisation, generator and phase, is constructed from the design alone, and against it are declared the priors of \autoref{tab:bayesPriors}, the thresholds of \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions} and the sensitivity ladder. Simulating responses from those priors on that skeleton, with no model output involved, constitutes a prior predictive check on the design actually used: it verifies that the priors are centred on no effect, that they admit effects large enough not to be shrunk away, and that the decision thresholds are reachable from either side.

\subsubsection{Observation}

This is the only stage that loads a trained network, and each geometry is loaded, measured and released in turn. Four families of measurement are collected, each answering a distinct question.

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
    \item \textbf{Forecast amplitude recovery.} For every triplet the median forecast is compared with the true continuation of the same signal, and the ratio of the fitted amplitudes at the frequency of interest is recorded with the phase error. Because the candidate and its two controls share a background realisation and a phase, their recoveries can be differenced into \autoref{eq:d2contrast}, the behavioural evidence about $H1$. The phase index is retained rather than averaged away, which is what makes $H2$ testable.
    \item \textbf{Frequency-local codelength.} The representational measurement is a minimum description length probe in the prequential form of Voita and Titov\autocite{voitaInformationTheoreticProbingMinimum2020}. \emph{What is probed} is the internal state at ten points of the network: the register token after each of the four encoder blocks, the decoder state after each of the four decoder blocks, the pre-projection vector and the projected quantile head. \emph{What is observed} is one codelength per cell, a cell being one probe stage, one geometry and one candidate frequency $f_c$, on the binary task of separating a tone at $f_c-1$\,Hz from one at $f_c+1$\,Hz. The examples of a cell are split into successive chunks: the first is charged at the uniform rate of one bit per example and each later chunk is encoded by a probe trained only on the chunks before it, at a cost of $-\log_2$ of the probability assigned to the true label. The total is the cost of transmitting the labels together with the probe that predicts them, so a probe that memorises noise pays for itself and gains nothing, which is why description length is preferred to accuracy. The task has constant difficulty at every frequency and the folds hold out phases rather than frequencies, so cells are comparable across the band, and a frequency whose neighbourhood has become linearly indistinguishable inside the network costs more bits whether or not the forecast recovers it. This is the representational evidence about $H1$.
    \item \textbf{Across-patch token dispersion.} Before any transformer block, the input patches of one context are embedded and the spread of those embeddings across patches is recorded. It is zero exactly when consecutive patches embed to the same vector, the degeneracy the stride condition predicts, and on a generated background it appears as a deep dip instead. It is swept on a grid that unions a uniform one-hertz sweep with the predicted sites of every geometry, so each is scored where a rival theory predicts a dip as well as where its own does. This is the location evidence for $H3$.
    \item \textbf{Detected sites and their spacing.} The dips of each profile are located, adjacent detections merged, and each site assigned to the branch that predicts it, with sites predicted by both set aside. The fundamental and the median spacing of each branch are the movement evidence for $H3a$ and $H3b$.
\end{itemize}

The generated backgrounds are archived alongside the measurements, so that the exact inputs behind any posterior can be reloaded; a Gaussian-process draw is reproducible only given its seed.

\subsubsection{Likelihood validation}

The likelihood is validated before it is trusted. Contrasts are simulated on the real design structure with the population effect fixed at four known values, spanning no attenuation, the $20\%$ reporting threshold, $50\%$ and $90\%$, and each scenario is refitted and compared with the known value. This distinguishes the two readings that a posterior centred on zero would otherwise admit: an effect that is absent, and a design unable to detect one.

\subsubsection{Posterior inference}

Every model is fitted under identical sampler settings, so that none receives a more or less careful exploration than another. For each hypothesis the credible interval of the estimand, the posterior probability of the preregistered threshold and the probability of practical equivalence are reported. Where competing formulations were defined they are compared by leave-one-out cross-validation: Model~A across covariate sets, which is where $M1$ is decided, Model~B with and without its corrections, Model~C with and without per-phase offsets, and Model~D1 across the four grid labellings.

\subsubsection{Checks and verdicts}

Convergence is verified for every fit against the diagnostics of \autoref{sec:appBayes}. Posterior predictive checks are run on Model~A, pooled and then stratified by configuration and by generator, because a hierarchical model can reproduce the marginal distribution of the contrast while systematically missing a stratum. Prior sensitivity is tested on the same model by refitting it across the scale ladder and with a Gaussian likelihood in place of the Student-$t_4$; the conclusion is reportable only if the decision probability and the credible interval remain stable across all variants, since otherwise what is reported is the prior rather than the data. Only then is the three-way rule of \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions} applied to each hypothesis, and the resulting verdict set beside the outcome an independent frequentist test reaches on the same phenomenon.
