Testing the hypotheses of \nameref{sec:four} requires a design in which the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ can be told apart, signals whose frequency content is known by construction, and an order of execution in which nothing that decides a hypothesis is chosen after the data have been seen.

\subsubsection{Patch geometries}

Chronos-Bolt-Tiny is distributed with the standard geometry $P=S=16$. Studying only that point would show how the published configuration behaves, but not whether a blind spot is specific to it or follows the tokenisation parameters that generate it. The design therefore varies $P$ and $S$ to observe Chronos beyond the standard configuration. At fixed $P$, changing $S$ varies the overlap and the spacing of the stride family; at fixed $S$, changing $P$ varies the patch width and the spacing of the patch family. The $P=S$ runs provide contiguous, non-overlapping baselines at different temporal resolutions. Configurations with $S>P$ are excluded because their gaps discard input samples and would confound tokenisation geometry with data loss.

\subsubsection{Retraining}

Every geometry, including the $P=S=16$ baseline, is trained from scratch. Changing $P$ changes the input dimension of the first patch-projection block, while changing $S$ changes the patch sequence on which the inter-patch dynamics are learned; reusing the published weights would therefore leave the variants on unequal training histories. Only the official Chronos-Bolt-Tiny architectural configuration is loaded, after which $(P,S)$ is overridden before random initialisation and the data, optimiser, budget and seed are held fixed across runs. The complete construction, streaming-data and optimisation procedure is given in \autoref{sec:appRetraining}.

\subsection{Analysis design}

All synthetic signals are sampled at $f_s=512$\,Hz and analysed over the band $[2,250]$\,Hz, strictly inside the Nyquist limit, whereas the telemetry is recorded once per minute, $f_{s-photo}=1/60$\,Hz; absolute frequencies are therefore not compared directly in hertz. The transferable quantity is instead the dimensionless position relative to the tokenisation geometry: in either setting, a candidate completes a whole number of cycles within one stride or one patch. Consequently, a result at $\mathrm{cpp}=k$ corresponds, for the one-minute series with $P=16$, to a component of period $16/k$ minutes. A single seed controls all stochastic generation.

\subsubsection{Exploratory frequency sweep}

Pure tones cross $[2,250]$\,Hz in one-hertz steps and at several phases. Their contexts are kept close to $512$ samples but shortened to a whole number of strides. For the behavioural reading, the median forecast is appended recursively until $512$ output samples have been generated, and the largest non-zero Fourier component gives the reconstructed frequency. For the representational reading, a linear probe acts on the output quantile head and is scored on two task families. The first is a seven hierarchical band-classification tasks, inspired by Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}, listed in \autoref{tab:bandTasks}. Each band asking on which side of a fixed boundary a tone lies, so that three levels of binary split place it in one of eight buckets; their folds are grouped by frequency, so a probe must learn the boundary rather than memorise individual tones. The second is a local task of constant difficulty at every frequency, distinguishing $f-1$\,Hz from $f+1$\,Hz, which holds out phases instead and carries no boundary artefact. The former records what the model generates; the latter records whether the frequency remains linearly readable before that output is formed. An example of the results provided by both the behavioural and representational described tasks is shown in \autoref{fig:rec16-16}.

\subsubsection{Bayesian analysis}

The Bayesian reading places a tracked tone on a unit-variance TSMixup or KernelSynth background at signal-to-noise ratio $4$. Every candidate $f_k$ is paired with controls $f_k\pm\delta$ that reuse its background and phase. The offset $\delta$ is the largest value not exceeding $0.25f_s/S$ that keeps both controls clear of the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$; a candidate with no admissible pair is omitted. Phase sampling follows the distinct alignments available on the sample grid, under the cap $S_f=\min\{f_s/\gcd(f,f_s)-1,\,N\}$ of Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}. Ten alignments are used per candidate for the paired contrasts and codelength. The four alignments used by the descriptive band tasks are averaged and do not enter these fits. Contexts contain $480$ samples and end on a stride boundary, so padding cannot be mistaken for a geometry effect; configurations that fail this condition remain descriptive.

Collection and fitting are kept separate. Priors and decision thresholds are fixed first, the likelihoods are checked by parameter recovery, and only then are the observations admitted to the models. One response filter is applied before fitting. A triplet is removed when $\max(R_k,R_-,R_+)\le 0.05$, because none of its arms rebuilds a measurable tone and its contrast is determined mainly by the stabilising constant of \autoref{eq:d2contrast}. The rule acts on the whole triplet, never on the candidate alone, so matched controls cannot be retained after their candidate is removed. Such rows would otherwise pull the population contrast towards zero; their exclusion therefore favours detection of an effect. The full likelihoods, priors and common sampler settings are given in \autoref{sec:appBayes}, and every posterior fit has its own checkpoint and sampling budget.

Model~A estimates forecast recovery at a candidate relative to its matched controls and partially pools that contrast across geometries. Its population ratio supplies the behavioural reading of $H1$, while overlap and log patch size explain the configuration effects and give the mitigation reading $M1$. The joint description is compared by leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO) with overlap only, patch size only and neither. Model~B measures the representational reading of $H1$ as the multiplicative change in frequency-local prequential codelength. Probe-stage and geometry offsets absorb their different baselines, and the adjusted form is compared with the literal unadjusted regression. Model~C retains phase by assigning each observation to one of eight bins. The spread of their offsets measures the phase dependence of the recovery deficit, and the phase-dependent fit is compared with the same model without those offsets.

Models~D1 and~D2 use token-dispersion measurements and retain pure signals as the exact-degeneracy reference for the two generated modes. Model~D1 fits each mode under four grid descriptions: both branches, stride only, patch only and neither. LOO then asks which description locates the observed dips. Model~D2 tests whether those sites move with the geometry. Each detection is assigned to the stride or patch branch, sites predicted by both are set aside, and each branch's detected fundamental is regressed on its predicted spacing. The median gap between successive detections is fitted alongside the fundamental as a fixed robustness reading.

Every estimand is returned to its natural scale and reported by its posterior median and $95\%$ credible interval, with the probability of its fixed threshold and, where defined, its region of practical equivalence. LOO is read with its uncertainty rather than by rank alone. Convergence, posterior predictive checks pooled and stratified by geometry and generator, and prior-scale and likelihood sensitivity fits gate the interpretation. A posterior that fails those checks remains inconclusive.
