Testing the hypotheses of \nameref{sec:four} requires a design in which the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ can be told apart, signals whose frequency content is known by construction, and an order of execution in which nothing that decides a hypothesis is chosen after the data have been seen.

\subsubsection{Patch geometries}

Chronos-Bolt-Tiny is distributed with the standard geometry $P=S=16$. Studying only that point would show how the published configuration behaves, but not whether a blind spot is specific to it or follows the tokenisation parameters that generate it. The design therefore varies $P$ and $S$ to observe Chronos beyond the standard configuration. At fixed $P$, changing $S$ varies the overlap and the spacing of the stride family; at fixed $S$, changing $P$ varies the patch width and the spacing of the patch family. The $P=S$ runs provide contiguous, non-overlapping baselines at different temporal resolutions. Configurations with $S>P$ are excluded because their gaps discard input samples and would confound tokenisation geometry with data loss.

\subsubsection{Retraining}

Every geometry, including the $P=S=16$ baseline, is trained from scratch. Changing $P$ changes the input dimension of the first patch-projection block, while changing $S$ changes the patch sequence on which the inter-patch dynamics are learned; reusing the published weights would therefore leave the variants on unequal training histories. Only the official Chronos-Bolt-Tiny architectural configuration is loaded, after which $(P,S)$ is overridden before random initialisation and the data, optimiser, budget and seed are held fixed across runs. The complete construction, streaming-data and optimisation procedure is given in \autoref{sec:appRetraining}.

\autoref{tab:hfModels} lists the retrained runs, $P$ and $S$ vary independently: series at fixed $P$ with varying $S$ isolate the stride, while series at fixed $S$ with varying $P$ isolate the patch. This is what lets Models~A, D1 and~D2 separate the two branches and estimate the overlap and patch-size covariates without confounding them. Six of the fifteen runs have $S \nmid P$, and those are the ones on which $\theta_S$ and $\kappa_S$ rest. All share the same training budget and seed, so a difference between them is a difference of geometry and not of training.

All synthetic signals are sampled at $f_s=512$\,Hz and analysed over the band $[2,250]$\,Hz, strictly inside the Nyquist limit, whereas the telemetry is recorded once per minute, $f_{s-photo}=1/60$\,Hz; absolute frequencies are therefore not compared directly in hertz. The transferable quantity is instead the dimensionless position relative to the tokenisation geometry: in either setting, a candidate completes a whole number of cycles within one stride or one patch. Consequently, a result at $\mathrm{cpp}=k$ corresponds, for the one-minute series with $P=16$, to a component of period $16/k$ minutes. A single seed controls all stochastic generation.

The two analyses deliberately use different signal sets. The exploratory sweep supplies pure sinusoids, whereas the paired Bayesian observations place the tracked tone on a unit-variance TSMixup or KernelSynth background at signal-to-noise ratio $4$. Each candidate $f_k$ shares its background and phase with controls $f_k\pm\delta$, where $\delta$ is the largest offset not exceeding $0.25f_s/S$ that leaves both controls clear of both branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$; a site with no admissible pair is dropped. Phases are drawn from the non-redundant alignments available at each frequency, under the cap $S_f=\min\{f_s/\gcd(f,f_s)-1,\,N\}$ of Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}, which replaces independently drawn random phases by the distinct alignments a tone can actually take on the sample grid. Ten are used per candidate for the paired contrasts and for the codelength cells, and four for the descriptive band tasks, where the phase axis is averaged rather than modelled. A frequency admitting fewer than the cap contributes the alignments it has. Bayesian contexts contain $480$ samples and must end on a stride boundary, since internal padding would otherwise be confounded with the geometry; configurations that fail this condition remain descriptive only.

One further exclusion applies to the paired observations themselves and is recorded here rather than left in the collection code, since it is a filter on the response. A triplet in which no arm is rebuilt at all, defined as $\max(R_k,R_-,R_+)\le 0.05$, is dropped: its contrast is a ratio of two noise floors, it carries no information about a difference between a candidate and its controls, and its logarithm is set by the stabilising constant of \autoref{eq:d2contrast} rather than by the data. The rule is evaluated on the triplet as a whole and never on the candidate arm alone, so it cannot remove candidates while keeping their controls, and it is applied before any model is fitted. Its direction is stated for completeness: a dropped triplet would enter with a contrast near zero, so retaining these rows would pull the population effect towards no effect. Excluding them therefore makes an effect easier to find, not harder, and any inconclusive behavioural result reported in \nameref{sec:seven} is not an artefact of the filter.

\subsubsection{Exploratory frequency sweep}

The descriptive workflow fixes one geometry at a time, measures and plots it, and releases it before loading the next. Pure tones cross $[2,250]$\,Hz in one-hertz steps and at several phases. Their contexts are kept close to $512$ samples but shortened to a whole number of strides. For the behavioural reading, the median forecast is appended recursively until $512$ output samples have been generated, and the largest non-zero Fourier component gives the reconstructed frequency. For the representational reading, a linear probe acts on the output quantile head and is scored on two task families. The first is the seven hierarchical band-classification tasks of \autoref{tab:bandTasks}, each asking on which side of a fixed boundary a tone lies, so that three levels of binary split place it in one of eight buckets; their folds are grouped by frequency, so a probe must learn the boundary rather than memorise individual tones. The second is a local task of constant difficulty at every frequency, distinguishing $f-1$\,Hz from $f+1$\,Hz, which holds out phases instead and carries no boundary artefact. The former records what the model generates; the latter records whether the frequency remains linearly readable before that output is formed.

A third descriptive reading is taken on generated rather than pure signals, and it is the one that speaks to the signal families of \nameref{sec:five} directly. One realisation is drawn, its context is given to the reference geometry, and the returned horizon is compared with the stretch of the real signal covering the same instants. Both sides are then decomposed: the amplitude and phase of each frequency are obtained by least squares at that known frequency, all frequencies of a page fitted jointly so that energy shared by two lines closer than the window can resolve is divided between them rather than counted twice, and the transforms against which the lines are checked are evaluated on a one-hertz grid by zero padding, which places a peak finely without adding resolving power. Which frequencies to fit is known by construction on the Light TSMixup side, since the generator records what it drew, and is read off the transform on the KernelSynth side, which has no lines to name. What the comparison contributes is the amplitude of each component after decomposition: a frequency present in the input and returned at a fraction of its amplitude is a different statement from one the model never returns at all, and the paired sweep readings above cannot separate the two.

Each reading is saved both per geometry and across the full geometry set, with the predicted sites superimposed. A tone reconstructed at the wrong frequency but still recoverable points to a loss after the representation; failure in both readings is the stronger frequency hole. The sweep remains descriptive because it averages over phase and supplies neither a likelihood nor uncertainty. Its reconstruction and recovery views reproduce the diagnostic of Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}; the pure-tone views of \autoref{sec:appSweep} show frequency holes on both readings, geometry by geometry. Whether they persist once the tone sits on a TSMixup or KernelSynth background is the question the inferential stage answers, which is why that stage uses the generated signals rather than treating the noise-free case as sufficient evidence. The resulting measurements are interpreted in \nameref{sec:seven}.

\subsubsection{Bayesian analysis}

The inferential stage begins only after the priors and decision thresholds of \nameref{sec:four} have been fixed, the observations collected and the likelihoods tested by parameter recovery. The full specifications and common sampler settings remain in \autoref{sec:appBayes}. Each posterior fit has its own checkpoint, so resumption cannot alter the design or give one model a different sampling budget. Models~A--C use tones carried by TSMixup and KernelSynth backgrounds; the location and movement tables used by D1 and D2 also retain the pure mode as the exact-degeneracy reference against which the two generated modes are read.

Model~A estimates the population recovery ratio at a candidate against its matched controls and the configuration-level variation used by $M1$. Its overlap-and-patch description is compared by leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO) with overlap only, patch size only and neither. Model~B estimates the multiplicative expansion of frequency-local prequential codelength and compares the stage-and-geometry-adjusted form with the literal unadjusted regression. Model~C estimates the spread of eight phase-bin offsets and compares the phase-dependent fit with its phase-free counterpart, separating the magnitude of a phase effect from whether including it predicts better.

Model~D1 asks which predicted grid locates the token-dispersion dips. For each signal mode, identical profiles are fitted under four labellings---both grids, stride only, patch only and neither---and ranked by LOO. Model~D2 then asks whether those sites move with the geometry: detections are assigned to the stride or patch branch, ambiguous sites are set aside, and each branch's fundamental is regressed on its predicted spacing. The median successive gap is fitted alongside as a robustness reading rather than substituted after inspection.

Every estimand is reported on its natural scale by its posterior median and $95\%$ credible interval, together with the probability of its preregistered threshold and, where defined, its region of practical equivalence. LOO is read through its uncertainty rather than by rank alone. Interpretation is then gated by convergence, posterior predictive checks pooled and stratified by configuration and generator, and the prior-scale and Gaussian-likelihood sensitivity fits. Only a posterior that passes those checks is carried into the three-way supported, refuted or inconclusive rule; numerical outcomes are reserved for \nameref{sec:seven}.
