Testing the hypotheses of \nameref{sec:four} requires a design in which the two branches of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$ can be told apart, signals whose frequency content is known by construction, and an order of execution in which nothing that decides a hypothesis is chosen after the data have been seen.

\subsubsection{Patch geometries}

Chronos-Bolt-Tiny is distributed with the standard geometry $P=S=16$. Studying only that point would show how the published configuration behaves, but not whether a blind spot follows the tokenisation parameters that generate it. The design therefore varies both: at fixed $P$, changing $S$ moves the overlap and the stride spacing; at fixed $S$, changing $P$ moves the patch width and the patch spacing, with the $P=S$ runs as contiguous baselines at different temporal resolutions. Configurations with $S>P$ are excluded, since their gaps discard input samples and would confound geometry with data loss.

\subsubsection{Retraining}

Every geometry, the baseline included, is trained from scratch: changing $P$ changes the input width of the first patch projection and changing $S$ the token sequence on which inter-patch dynamics are learned, so reusing the published weights would leave the variants on unequal training histories. Only the architectural configuration is taken from the published model; $(P,S)$ is overridden before random initialisation, and data, optimiser, budget and seed are held fixed. \autoref{sec:appRetraining} gives the construction, the streaming data and the optimisation.

\subsection{Analysis design}

Synthetic signals are sampled at $f_s=512$\,Hz and analysed over $[2,250]$\,Hz, strictly inside the Nyquist limit, whereas the telemetry is recorded once per minute, so absolute frequencies are not comparable in hertz. The transferable quantity is dimensionless: a candidate completes a whole number of cycles within one stride or one patch, so $\mathrm{cpp}=k$ corresponds, for a one-minute series with $P=16$, to a period of $16/k$ minutes. A single seed controls all stochastic generation and trains every geometry, so the design does not separate a geometry from the instance trained at it, and a small effect could be seed-dependent.

\subsubsection{Exploratory frequency sweep}

Pure tones cross the band in one-hertz steps at several phases, on contexts near $512$ samples shortened to a whole number of strides. The behavioural reading appends the median forecast recursively to $512$ generated samples and takes the largest non-zero Fourier component as the reconstructed frequency, recording what the model produces. The representational reading trains a linear probe on a reduced subspace of the output quantile head and asks whether the frequency stays linearly readable before that output is formed. It is scored on the seven hierarchical band-classification tasks of \autoref{tab:bandTasks}, after Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}, whose three levels of binary split place a tone in one of eight buckets, and on a local task of constant difficulty everywhere separating $f-1$\,Hz from $f+1$\,Hz. \autoref{sec:appSweep} states how each is scored and \autoref{fig:rec16-16} shows both. The scan is on the integer grid and evaluates no lock condition exactly: where a geometry has non-integer candidates it resolves their neighbourhood rather than the site. Averaging over phase and carrying no controls, its purpose is not to test a hypothesis but to establish whether the model represents frequency content at all and where that ability degrades. The inferential stage below places every candidate and control at its exact value instead.

\subsubsection{Bayesian analysis}

The inferential reading places a tracked tone on a unit-variance TSMixup or KernelSynth background at signal-to-noise ratio $4$. Each candidate $f_k$ is paired with controls $f_k\pm\delta$ reusing its background and phase, where $\delta$ is the largest offset not exceeding $0.25f_s/S$ that keeps both controls clear of either branch; a candidate with no admissible pair is dropped. Phases are the distinct alignments the sample grid admits, under the cap $S_f=\min\{f_s/\gcd(f,f_s)-1,\,N\}$ of Pagani et al.\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}, ten per candidate. Every geometry receives the same $480$-sample context. Since $480$ is a multiple of every patch size used, no padding is inserted; the patch grid then spans $L_{\mathrm{tok}}=P+S\lfloor(480-P)/S\rfloor$ samples and the final $r=(480-P)\bmod S$ are carried into no token. Exact tiling requires $(L-P)\bmod S=0$, which under a context divisible by $S$ holds precisely when $S \mid P$: the nine geometries with $S \mid P$ have $r=0$, while the six with $S \nmid P$ discard between four and sixteen of the most recent samples. \autoref{tab:hfModels} gives both quantities per geometry.

The truncation cancels in the paired contrast, which compares a candidate with its own controls under one geometry, but not between geometries; \autoref{sec:appBayes} states what it does and does not confound, and it is reported rather than adjusted for.

The design is fixed before anything is measured: the configurations, the frequency grid and the coverage each geometry must reach are declared once, and a session collecting only part of it records that as a limitation rather than a new design. Each background is archived with its recipe and a content hash and admitted only if both still agree, and the collected rows are checked against the declared design, for unambiguous sites per branch and usable rows per geometry, before any model is fitted.

One response filter is applied at that point. A triplet is removed when $\max(R_k,R_-,R_+)\le 0.05$, since no arm rebuilds a measurable tone and its contrast is then set by the stabilising constant of \autoref{eq:d2contrast} rather than by the data. The rule acts on the whole triplet, so a control cannot survive its candidate, and such rows would pull the population contrast towards zero, so removing them favours detection.

\autoref{tab:bayesModels} pairs each claim with the model that decides it and \autoref{sec:appBayes} states the likelihoods, priors and sampler settings. Model~A pools the paired contrast across geometries and carries overlap and log patch size at its configuration level, so $M1$ is a reading of the same fit rather than a separate one. Models~B, C and~D1 each have a nested counterpart, unadjusted, phase-free and single-grid, compared by leave-one-out cross-validation read with its uncertainty rather than by rank. Model~D2 assigns each detection to one branch and sets aside the sites both grids predict. Both D1 and D2 are fitted on token dispersion, which is a property of the tokenisation: a collapse of consecutive token embeddings at $f=c f_s/S$ follows from the arithmetic and would appear in an untrained network. They therefore locate candidate sites, and the evidence that a trained model loses information there is carried by Models~A and~B, which are measured on the forecast and on the representation against matched controls.

Interpretation is fail-closed. Convergence, parameter recovery for every likelihood family, posterior predictive adequacy pooled and stratified by geometry and generator, prior and likelihood sensitivity, and the reliability of the leave-one-out estimates are explicit gates, fixed with the thresholds of \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions}. A gate that fails does not weaken a claim: the estimand is reported as not reportable, and a branch with fewer than ten unambiguous sites as not identified, neither of which is a null result. Every estimand is returned to its natural scale and reported by its posterior median and $95\%$ credible interval, with the probability of its threshold and, where defined, its region of practical equivalence.
