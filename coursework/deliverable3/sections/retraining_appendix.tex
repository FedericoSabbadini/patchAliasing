The retraining procedure was first specified for the five-geometry design of Deliverable~1 and is implemented in \texttt{chronos/models/train\_sweep.py}. The present analysis extends the same controlled comparison to the fifteen geometries in \autoref{tab:hfModels}. At sweep level the geometry list is the only change: \texttt{PS\_GRID} is populated with the required $(P,S)$ pairs, crossed with seed $42$, and processed sequentially. Each run is written to its own directory, such as \texttt{p16-s8-seed42}, so checkpoints and diagnostics cannot be mixed between geometries.

\subsection{Model construction}

The script obtains the architectural configuration of \texttt{amazon/chronos-bolt-tiny}, but does not load its pretrained weights. It preserves the encoder and decoder dimensions, attention heads and layer counts, fixes the context and prediction lengths at $2048$ and $64$ samples, and retains the nine forecast quantiles from $0.1$ to $0.9$. Before instantiation it replaces \texttt{input\_patch\_size} by $P$ and \texttt{input\_patch\_stride} by $S$, then constructs a randomly initialised \texttt{ChronosBoltModelForForecasting}.

Training from scratch is necessary for $P$ because the first projection receives both the $P$ patch values and their $P$ observation-mask entries, so its input width is $2P$ and weights from a different patch size are dimensionally incompatible. A change in $S$ does not resize that projection, but it changes which windows become neighbouring tokens, their overlap and the sequence length seen by the transformer. Retraining the stride variants as well prevents a comparison between a model adapted to one token sequence and another trained on the sequence under test. The standard $P=S=16$ model is therefore retrained under the same budget rather than represented by the larger published checkpoint.

\subsection{Streaming training data}

The training data follow the Chronos pretraining mixture\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}: the $10$-million-series TSMixup corpus and the $1$-million-series KernelSynth corpus are loaded from \texttt{autogluon/chronos\_datasets} as streaming datasets, independently shuffled with seed $42$, and interleaved in a $9{:}1$ ratio. The shuffle buffer is $10{,}000$ examples rather than the published $100{,}000$, the smaller value adopted in Deliverable~1 to avoid streaming timeouts.

For each series the iterator first requires at least $60+64$ samples, leaving a minimum of $60$ past positions before the $64$-sample forecast horizon, and rejects a series if more than $90\%$ of its values are missing. It then draws a missing-value rate uniformly between $0$ and $0.2$, replaces that fraction of observations with \texttt{NaN}, and samples one valid split point. The most recent $2048$ past values form the context; shorter histories are left-padded with \texttt{NaN}. The next $64$ values form the target. Boolean masks accompany both arrays, and windows with an entirely missing context or target are discarded. This preserves missingness through the model's normalisation step while excluding unobserved target positions from the quantile loss.

\subsection{Optimisation and saved artefacts}

Each geometry is trained for exactly $100{,}000$ gradient updates with batches of $32$. The optimiser is AdamW, using its fused CUDA implementation where available, with learning rate $10^{-3}$, zero weight decay and gradient norm clipped at $1$. The learning rate decays linearly to zero with no warmup. Computation remains in \texttt{fp32}; TF32 matrix multiplication is enabled on compatible NVIDIA devices. These settings reproduce the published Chronos-T5 recipe used as the reference in Deliverable~1, except for the common $100$k rather than $200$k step budget and the reduced shuffle buffer. Applying both departures to every geometry, including the baseline, keeps them outside the $(P,S)$ contrast.

The loop logs loss every $50$ steps and saves a model snapshot every $1{,}000$ steps. At completion it stores \texttt{loss\_history.npy}, \texttt{config.json} and \texttt{model.safetensors}, then writes a \texttt{DONE} marker. Later invocations skip completed directories; an exception leaves that geometry incomplete without stopping the sweep, and no optimiser or scheduler state is reloaded automatically.
