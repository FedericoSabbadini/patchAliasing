This section separates what the signals show from what the inferential stage decides. The first three readings are exploratory: they point to where frequency content behaves differently, and the Bayesian analysis of \autoref{sec:bayesResults} tests that direction under the rules of \autoref{tab:bayesDecisions}.

\subsection{The frequency sweep}

Across the fifteen geometries, all drawn in \autoref{sec:appSweep}, the forecast rebuilds a pure tone at the right frequency only over the lower part of the band, and above it mostly returns slow content unrelated to the input; the rebuilt frequency lies within two sweep steps of the input for only $7$ to $15\%$ of the tones. The exceptions point at the candidate sites: inputs near $f_s/4$, a site of every geometry, are returned at $f_s/4$, inputs on a site often make the output jump, and the reading is least stable across phases on those plateaus. The linear probes behave differently. The local task reaches an accuracy of $0.9$ over $66$ to $91\%$ of the band, least at p32-s32, including where the forecast fails, while the band tasks weaken beside their own boundaries, which lie next to sites shared by every geometry, so a boundary effect cannot be told from a site effect. \autoref{fig:rec16-16} shows both readings at the reference geometry. The common case is thus a frequency that the representation retains and the output misplaces, with the sites acting on the output as attractors rather than holes; this gives the study its direction but confirms none of the claims of \autoref{sec:agent}.

\subsection{Recovering a component from a complex background}

Tones added to a Light TSMixup or KernelSynth background are seldom returned by the retrained models, and the result over a large sample of draws was unexpected. The injected tone is much harder to recover at the control frequencies than at the candidate sites: it reappears in about a quarter of the trials on a site and almost never beside one, and still in a sixth to a fifth once the lines a forecast draws there with no tone are discounted (\autoref{tab:decomposition}; procedure and single draws in \autoref{sec:appDecomp}). The pattern is the same for both generators, with the smallest patch retaining the most and the widest the least, which points to the geometry rather than to the signal family; the published checkpoint returns added components more often on and off the sites. The result runs against the loss that $H1$ predicts and agrees with the attraction seen in the sweep. Two mechanisms may contribute: a tone on a candidate site produces a repeating token sequence (\autoref{sec:tokenisation}) that a forecaster can prolong without representing the frequency, and the forecasts carry lines of their own on the sites, which a count of presence credits as recovered and which account for a quarter to two fifths of the lock recoveries. Whether a loss concentrates at the sites once background and phase are matched is what the Bayesian analysis was designed to decide.

\subsection{Prediction on the measured series}

On the photovoltaic series every checkpoint forecasts the same windows across the year and is scored on the same minutes (\autoref{tab:solarPrediction}; procedure in \autoref{sec:appSolar}). The error concentrates in daylight and in the transitions around sunrise and sunset, while at night almost every model correctly continues a series held at zero; the forecasts examined prolong the level of the last hour when the measured power has already begun to change. The published checkpoint is the most accurate, more plausibly because of its training than of its geometry. Among the retrained models none improves on the $P=S=16$ baseline, several cannot be told apart from it and the rest are worse, with no ordering by overlap or patch size; p32-s20 has the largest mean absolute error.

The series informs the application rather than testing the design: the dominant content of every window is the daily cycle, far below the first candidate site at one cycle per patch, so no window carries appreciable energy on $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}$. Content at the sites reaches this plant only through weather transients lasting a few minutes, the events a confirmed loss at the sites would leave the forecaster least able to represent.

Every one of the $41$ fits converged, with $\hat R$ at most $1.004$, an effective sample size above $1700$ and no divergence, and no decision class moves over the prior-sensitivity ladder. Before the other gates the posteriors read clearly (\autoref{tab:bayesEstimates}, \autoref{fig:priorPosterior}). At a candidate frequency the forecast recovers more of the tone than at its controls, not less: $e^{\bar\beta}=1.35$ $[1.25,1.45]$, and $1.38$ once the lines the model draws with no tone are removed, so the rule of $H1$ on the forecast would refute it. The gain varies far more between candidate frequencies than between geometries, a scale of $1.2$ on the log ratio against $0.14$. In the internal state the codelength at a candidate grows by $6\%$ $[4,8]$, inside the $10\%$ equivalence band, which would refute $H1$ on the representation. The effect barely changes with phase, $\sigma_\phi=0.006$, which would support $H2$, although leave-one-out still detects a phase dependence too small to matter. The overlap slope is $\delta_O=-0.13$ $[-0.29,0.03]$, with $\Pr(\delta_O>0)=0.053$, and leave-one-out prefers the model without it, so $M1$ would be inconclusive at the edge of refutation. The patch-size slope, $\delta_P=-0.05$ $[-0.26,0.16]$, is not resolved, since recovery shows that this design cannot tell a slope of $-0.15$ from none. The token dispersion dips on the stride grid, $\theta_S=-0.26$ and $-0.38$ on the two generators, but not on the patch grid, $|\theta_P|<0.02$, as expected where the arithmetic makes consecutive patches identical only on the stride comb; $H3$ location, which needs both, would therefore be refuted. The stride sites move exactly as predicted, $\kappa_S=1.000$ $[0.990,1.011]$, which would support $H3a$, while no patch-only site was unambiguous and $H3b$ is not identified. \autoref{fig:priorPosterior} shows every posterior far narrower than its prior and well away from it, so these readings are set by the data rather than by the priors; only $\delta_O$ straddles the boundary of its rule.

These readings are not verdicts, because each rests on conditions the gates test. Convergence establishes that the chains explored the posterior they report. Parameter recovery shows that a model, on this design, finds a known effect and returns a null when none is present. The posterior predictive check asks whether the fitted model reproduces the data stratum by stratum, since an estimand is only as credible as the model that defines it. Prior sensitivity shows that a conclusion belongs to the data rather than to the prior, the reliability gate shows that the comparisons behind $M1$ and $H3$ location rest on stable importance weights, and the agreement gates show that no conclusion depends on which of two defensible readings is used.

Convergence and sensitivity hold for every claim, and agreement wherever both readings exist; the predictive check fails for all of them, and recovery for Models~A, B and~C and for the patch branch of D2, so no claim is reportable and $H3b$ is not identified (\autoref{tab:bayesGates}). The models place the stratum means close to the data, typically within $4\%$ of a stratum's standard deviation, but not its dispersion (\autoref{fig:ppcDispersion}). Model~A has one residual scale for fifteen geometries whose standard deviation ranges from $0.6$ to $1.8$, and a Student-$t$ likelihood cannot reproduce the skew of a contrast whose mean is $0.32$ and median $0.02$; with $371{,}000$ triplets, the replicated intervals are narrow enough for any such mismatch to fail. The recovery failures are narrower (\autoref{sec:appBayes}). Reading the estimates as verdicts would assume the adequacy the gates were placed to test, so they remain the direction of the evidence rather than a decision on it.
