\noindent Patch-based tokenisation is the standard input stage of time-series foundation models. This report shows that it introduces a redundancy classical sampling theory does not describe. When a signal completes a whole number of cycles within one stride, consecutive tokens repeat, and within one patch the token sequence repeats at a longer spacing, so the frequency may become poorly observable to the model although the sampler represented it perfectly. Patch tokenisation is modelled as an operator, and the frequencies at which tokens repeat form a closed-form set, fixed by the sampling rate, the patch size and the stride and computable without model access. The candidate set is thus a property of the configuration, not of the data.

\noindent Fifteen geometries of Chronos-Bolt-Tiny are retrained from scratch under one budget so that patch size and stride vary independently, and the recovery of a tone is measured against matched control frequencies, in the forecast and in the internal representation, under pre-registered Bayesian rules. A photovoltaic control setting supplies the semantic layer through a formal ontology, stating the consequence in domain terms.

\noindent A risk assessment under the AI Risk Management Framework of the National Institute of Standards and Technology treats the phenomenon as a candidate systematic vulnerability: if confirmed, a blind spot whose sites are derivable in closed form would be invisible to an operator who never derived them and reachable by an adversary without model access. Patch geometry becomes a deployment parameter, to be chosen against the known target spectral content rather than inherited as a default; and any monitor meant to catch what the model misses must act on the raw signal upstream of the tokeniser, since one placed downstream would inherit any blind spot the tokeniser induces.

\noindent Before the pre-registered gates, the posteriors point away from a blind spot: at a candidate frequency the forecast recovers a tone $35\%$ better than beside it, $[25,45]\%$, and the internal state needs only $6\%$ more code to read it. The effect barely changes with phase, the stride sites move exactly as predicted, and overlap brings no measurable mitigation, none of which is yet a verdict. Every model converged, but none reproduces the dispersion of the data and three also fail parameter recovery, so no claim is reportable, and the risk assessment keeps every exposure that depends on a loss open while treating the candidate set as a monitoring target.
