\noindent Patch-based tokenisation is the standard input stage of time-series foundation models, adopted for efficiency. This report shows that it introduces a distortion classical sampling theory does not describe. When a signal completes a whole number of cycles within one stride or one patch, consecutive tokens repeat, and the frequency becomes poorly observable to the model although the sampler represented it perfectly. Patch tokenisation is modelled as an operator, the frequencies at which the degeneracy occurs are derived as a closed-form set determined by the sampling rate, the patch size and the stride, and that set is shown to be computable without model access. The significance is that this is a property of the configuration, not of the data.

\noindent Fifteen geometries of Chronos-Bolt-Tiny are retrained from scratch under one budget so that patch size and stride vary independently, and the resulting loss is measured against matched control frequencies, in the forecast and in the internal representation, under pre-registered Bayesian decision rules. A photovoltaic control setting supplies the semantic layer through a formal ontology, so the consequence can be stated in domain terms.

\noindent An assessment based on NIST AI RMF treats the phenomenon as a candidate systematic vulnerability: a blind spot derivable in closed form is one an operator cannot see without deriving it and an adversary can reach without model access.The impact is a design consequence. Patch geometry becomes a deployment parameter, to be chosen against the known target spectral content rather than inherited as a default; and any monitor meant to catch what the model misses must act on the raw signal upstream of the tokeniser, since one placed downstream inherits the blind spot it is meant to find.
