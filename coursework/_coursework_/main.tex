	%---------------------------------------- ABSTRACT -------------------------------------------
	\begin{mdframed}[style=theoremstyle, linecolor=rulegray]
		\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}\quad
		Patch-based tokenisation is a cornerstone of modern time-series foundation models, enabling
		transformers to process long sequences efficiently by grouping consecutive samples into
		fixed-length patches.
		This work investigates a representational failure mode that we term \emph{structural aliasing}:
		when the stride of the patch grid is commensurate with the period of an input signal,
		consecutive tokens become identical, collapsing the token sequence to a low-rank representation
		and rendering the model blind to that frequency component.
		We formalise patch tokenisation as a linear operator, derive the set of blind-spot frequencies
		as harmonics of the patch frequency $f_{\mathrm{patch}} = f_s/S$, and show that this failure
		is orthogonal to classical Nyquist–Shannon undersampling.
		We further connect signal-level observability loss to domain-level concept distortions through
		an ontological semantic layer, validate three falsifiable hypotheses via controlled synthetic
		experiments, quantify uncertainty using Bayesian inference, and investigate a candidate
		mitigation strategy.
		Finally, we discuss how the findings can inform the design of agentic AI systems that monitor,
		explain, and act upon structural aliasing risks in real-time deployments.
	\end{mdframed}
