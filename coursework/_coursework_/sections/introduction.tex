Time-series forecasting underpins operational decision-making wherever a system's future state must be anticipated from its past, from grid load to industrial condition monitoring: projecting historical sequences into future horizons is what allows a control policy to be proactive rather than reactive\autocite{hyndmanForecastingPrinciplesPractice2021}.

\subsection{Evolution of Forecasting Methodologies}
Classical time-series analysis relies on statistical models such as AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) and Exponential Smoothing (ETS)\autocite{hyndmanForecastingPrinciplesPractice2021}. These are interpretable and robust on stable low-dimensional data, but they assume linearity and stationarity and are fitted to one series at a time.

Deep learning relaxed those assumptions: recurrent networks and their gated variants\autocite{hochreiterLongShortTermMemory1997} model non-linear dynamics through a hidden state, and training jointly across many related series established the global forecasting paradigm\autocite{benidisDeepLearningTime2022}. Transformers then replaced the recurrent bottleneck with self-attention\autocite{vaswaniAttentionAllYou2017}, computing dependencies between distant time steps directly. Two costs follow for sampled signals: attention scales quadratically with sequence length, and a single time step is a weak unit of meaning. Patching addresses both, segmenting the series into contiguous sub-sequences and embedding each as one token, which shortens the sequence and gives every token a locally semantic support\autocite{nieTimeSeriesWorth2023}.

Time-series foundation models sit at the end of this line. Chronos treats time-series values as discrete tokens obtained by scaling and quantisation\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024}; its successor Chronos-Bolt replaces quantisation with the patching scheme above and a direct multi-quantile head\autocite{ansariChronosLearningLanguage2024a}.\footnote{The vendor reports up to a $250\times$ inference speed-up over the original Chronos\autocite{FastAccurateZeroshot2024a}; no peer-reviewed description of Chronos-Bolt has been published.} Patching is therefore not an incidental implementation detail, but the design choice that makes this class of models practical at scale.

\subsection{Classic Aliasing}
Whatever the architecture downstream, predictive fidelity is bounded by the digital representation of the signal. The governing limit is the Nyquist--Shannon sampling theorem\autocite{shannonCommunicationPresenceNoise1949}: a band-limited continuous-time signal is perfectly reconstructible only if sampled uniformly at a rate $f_s$ strictly greater than twice its highest frequency component $f_{\max}$,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nyquist_criterion}
f_s > 2f_{\max}.
\end{equation}

The threshold $f_N = f_s/2$ is the Nyquist frequency. When the criterion is violated, through an insufficient sampling rate or a missing anti-aliasing filter, components above $f_N$ fold back into the lower spectrum and become indistinguishable from genuine low-frequency content\autocite{oppenheimDiscreteTimeSignalProcessing2010}. This is \textit{aliasing}, and it is irreversible: the information is destroyed at acquisition, not at inference. Respecting \autoref{eq:nyquist_criterion} is consequently taken as sufficient for a discrete sequence to represent its source faithfully. This report asks whether that sufficiency survives patch-based tokenization.

\subsection{Application Domain and Scope}
The reference setting is a designed scenario: a photovoltaic array supplying an internal load, a local storage battery and the national grid, with a control agent performing load balancing, battery dispatch, state monitoring and anomaly detection on short-horizon forecasts. Measured photovoltaic and weather telemetry are taken from the SolarTech Lab dataset\autocite{levaPhotovoltaicPowerWeather2020}; the concepts, states and relations of the scenario are formalised as an OWL2 ontology in \nameref{sec:two}.% \autoref once `unnumberedsections` is removed from main.tex

The target architecture is Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, with default patch size $P=16$, stride $S=16$, context window $T=2048$, four encoder and four decoder blocks, and a direct quantile projection head\autocite{AmazonChronosbolttinyHugging2025}. The objective is to test the assumption that such a model preserves the frequency content of Nyquist-compliant signals, and to characterise how $f_s$, $P$ and $S$ interact in determining when it does not\autocite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}.
