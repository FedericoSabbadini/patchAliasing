The considered domain takes into account the energy generated by an asset infrastructure centered around a \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray}, operating within a localized smart micro-grid ecosystem. This environment is dynamically monitored, analyzed, and managed by an autonomous AI \texttt{ControlAgent} that balances power flows, detects system failures, and evaluates data integrity. Uniquely, the domain bridges physical power generation with data science tokenization constraints, mapping how time-series windowing chunking strategies (\textbf{Chronos patch tokenization}) impact the data fidelity fed into machine learning models.

\subsection{Physical Assets and Grid Topology}
The physical core of the system is composed of four primary entities under the \texttt{PhysicalComponent} class, which are physically \texttt{connectedTo} one another to form the micro-grid's electrical topology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray} serves as the primary generation source, defined by its physical size (\texttt{panelCount}) and inverter nameplate capacity (\texttt{nominalPowerW}).
    \item The array directly \texttt{suppliesTo} three downstream assets: the \texttt{NationalGrid} interface (an infinite bidirectional bus for energy exchange), local \texttt{InternalLoad} units (representing dynamic consumption demand), and a \texttt{BatteryStorage} unit.
    \item Functionally, the \texttt{BatteryStorage} unit actively \texttt{buffers} the \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray}, absorbing excess generation or discharging power during deficits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Telemetry Streams and Tokenization Layer}
To evaluate system performance, data streams are continuously collected across two operational vectors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray} \texttt{measures} a continuous \texttt{Signal}, which represents the raw time-series telemetry stream. This stream encapsulates instantaneous data properties such as photovoltaic power (\texttt{pvPowerW}), global horizontal irradiance (\texttt{horizontalIrradianceWm2}), and tilted surface irradiance (\texttt{tiltedIrradianceWm2}), alongside frequency-domain metadata including the raw sampling frequency, fundamental wave frequency, amplitude, and phase.
    \item Concurrently, the array is mapped via a \texttt{hasWeatherObservation} relationship to a \texttt{WeatherObservation} snapshot, which logs environmental metrics like ambient air temperature (\texttt{airTemperatureC}) and wind vectors.
\end{itemize}
To prepare this continuous telemetry for deep learning inference, the raw \texttt{Signal} passes through a processing layer that \texttt{tokenizes} it into a \texttt{PatchedSignal}. This represents the sequence chunking strategy employed by foundation architectures like Chronos, configured via discrete attributes: \texttt{patchSize} (samples per token), \texttt{stride} (step size between windows), and an \texttt{overlapRatio}.

\subsection{Inferred Environmental Contexts and System Anomalies}
An execution reasoner dynamically updates the operational metadata of the system based on physical and mathematical rules. The system extracts a baseline \texttt{GenerationState} derived entirely from the tilted solar irradiance ($G_{\text{tilt}}$) striking the panels, classifying the micro-grid into one of four mutually exclusive operating environments: \texttt{ClearSkyState} (peak production), \texttt{PartialCloudState} (intermittent volatility), \texttt{OvercastState} (dense cloud cover), or \texttt{NighttimeState} (zero solar availability).

Simultaneously, the system assesses physical health by evaluating whether the \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray} \texttt{hasAnomaly}. It detects six distinct \texttt{AnomalyState} subclasses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clipping Anomaly:} Occurs when solar power saturates and hits the inverter's maximum limits under intense irradiance.
    \item \textbf{Inverter Trip Anomaly:} A critical physical failure where power output drops to zero despite high available solar irradiance.
    \item \textbf{Ramp Anomaly:} Detected when the rate of change in power ($\Delta P$) over a consecutive interval exceeds safe grid-frequency thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Soiling Anomaly:} A prolonged drop in conversion efficiency caused by physical panel surface contamination (dust, soil).
    \item \textbf{Overheating Anomaly:} High ambient temperatures trigger thermal derating, decreasing panel performance under strong sunlight.
    \item \textbf{Malfunction Anomaly:} Severe degradation where hardware output drops to a critically low, non-zero threshold without a clear environmental cause.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Machine Learning Vulnerabilities: Aliasing Events}
In parallel with physical faults, the system evaluates data-processing anomalies. If the chosen patch tokenization parameters conflict with the signal's natural periodic cycles, the \texttt{PatchedSignal} logically \texttt{exhibitsAliasing}, triggering an \texttt{AliasingEvent}. These represent structural blind spots where information is permanently lost prior to AI forecasting:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A \texttt{FrequencyBlindSpot} occurs when the signal's fundamental frequency (or its secondary harmonic) matches the patch-induced frequency ($f_p = f_s / S$), rendering cyclical trends invisible to the model (Hypotheses H1 and H1b).
    \item A \texttt{PhaseAliasingEffect} (Hypothesis H2) is flagged when a temporal phase offset misaligns with the rigid token boundaries ($0 < \phi < S$), corrupting early representation features.
    \item An \texttt{OverlapEffect} (Hypothesis H3) identifies configuration gaps where a low-density overlap ratio ($\gamma < 0.5$) introduces data redundancy or framing distortion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Autonomous Action}
To maintain grid equilibrium and mitigate faults, the \texttt{PhotovoltaicArray} \texttt{triggersAction} directives that map directly to an \texttt{AgentAction}. The autonomous \texttt{ControlAgent} is responsible for orchestrating the closed-loop feedback loop and \texttt{performs} four targeted interventions based on the inferred states:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It schedules a \texttt{BatteryDispatchAction} to buffer power during \texttt{PartialCloudState} volatility or to substitute missing capacity during an \texttt{InverterTripAnomaly}.
    \item It executes a \texttt{CurtailGenerationAction} to throttle the inverter during \texttt{ClippingAnomaly} or \texttt{OverheatingAnomaly} events to safeguard structural integrity.
    \item It implements a \texttt{LoadAdjustmentAction} to dynamically down-shift internal consumer demand during \texttt{OvercastState} or \texttt{NighttimeState} deficits.
    \item It fires a \texttt{RampAlertAction} to warn the broader grid stability network the moment a dangerous \texttt{RampAnomaly} is diagnosed.
\end{itemize}
