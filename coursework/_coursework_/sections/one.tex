Let $\mathbf{x} = \{x[n]\}_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}$ be a discrete-time signal representing the input time series. We define the patch-based tokenization operator $\mathcal{T}_{P,S}$ with patch length $P \in \mathbb{N}^{+}$ and stride $S \in \mathbb{N}^{+}$ as a mapping that transforms the signal $\mathbf{x}$ into a sequence of vector-valued tokens $\mathbf{T}=\{\mathbf{t}_k\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}$, where each token $\mathbf{t}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$.

The $k$-th token is defined as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:token_def}
\mathbf{t}_k
=
\mathcal{T}_{P,S}(\mathbf{x})[k]
=
\begin{bmatrix}
x[kS] \\
x[kS+1] \\
\vdots \\
x[kS+P-1]
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Using element-wise indexing notation,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:element_def}
\mathbf{t}_k[m]
=
x[kS+m],
\qquad
m\in\{0,\ldots,P-1\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Phase-Locking and Patch Indistinguishability}
Consider a continuous-time periodic signal sampled uniformly at sampling frequency $f_s$, yielding the discrete-time sequence $x[n]$. We isolate a fundamental harmonic component and model it as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sinusoid}
x[n]
=
\sin\!\left(
2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}n+\phi
\right),
\end{equation}

where $f$ denotes the signal frequency satisfying the Nyquist criterion $f < \frac{f_s}{2}$, and $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ is an arbitrary phase offset.

Phase-locking occurs when the structural phase profile of the signal repeats exactly under a translational shift equal to the stride $S$. Evaluating a stride translation yields

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stride_translation}
x[n+S]
=
\sin\!\left(
2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}(n+S)+\phi
\right)
=
\sin\!\left(
2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}n
+
2\pi\frac{fS}{f_s}
+
\phi
\right).
\end{equation}

For exact alignment between the signal and the tokenization operator, the phase accumulation over one stride must correspond to an integer number of $2\pi$--periods

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase_boundary}
2\pi\frac{fS}{f_s}
=
2\pi c,
\qquad
c\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.
\end{equation}

As a result, the signal exhibits exact stride-period translational invariance

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stride_translation_invariance}
x[n+S]
=
\sin\!\left(
2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}n
+
2\pi c
+
\phi
\right)
=
\sin\!\left(
2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}n
+\phi
\right)
=
x[n].
\end{equation}

The same derivation can be applied taking into account translational shifts equal to the patch size $P$, yielding a similar phase-locking condition

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase_boundary_2}
2\pi\frac{fP}{f_s}
=
2\pi k
\Rightarrow
x[n+P]
=
x[n],
\qquad
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.
\end{equation}

Solving \autoref{eq:phase_boundary} and \autoref{eq:phase_boundary_2} for $f$ yields the phase-locking frequency class $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lock}}$, which is defined as the set of frequencies producing self-repetition over one stride or over one patch.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flock_set}
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{lock}}
=
\left\{
f\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
\;\middle|\;
f
=
c\frac{f_s}{S}
\lor
f
=
k\frac{f_s}{P},
\;
c, k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},
\;
f<\frac{f_s}{2}
\right\}.
\end{equation}

It is convenient to express both conditions in units that do not depend on the sampling rate. Introducing the cycles-per-stride and cycles-per-patch ratios

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp}
\mathrm{cps}
=
\frac{f S}{f_s},
\qquad
\mathrm{cpp}
=
\frac{f P}{f_s},
\end{equation}

\autoref{eq:phase_boundary} and \autoref{eq:phase_boundary_2} read simply $\mathrm{cps}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}$ and $\mathrm{cpp}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}$: $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lock}}$ is the set of frequencies completing a whole number of cycles within one stride or within one patch. This normalisation makes the two branches directly comparable across geometries and across sampling rates.

The collapse of consecutive tokens is not restricted to sinusoidal signals. Consider an arbitrary discrete-time signal satisfying the translational invariance condition

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:general_periodicity}
x[n+S]
=
x[n],
\qquad
\forall n.
\end{equation}

Taking into account two consecutive tokens, using the element-wise notation from \autoref{eq:element_def} and applying \autoref{eq:general_periodicity} gives

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{t}_{k+1}[m]
=
x[(k+1)S+m]
=
x[kS+m+S]
=
x[kS+m]
=
\mathbf{t}_k[m].
\end{equation}

Since this equality holds for every component,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:general_token_collapse}
\mathbf{t}_{k+1}
=
\mathbf{t}_k.
\end{equation}

The derivation above shows that some translations leave a signal unchanged; this repetition is not, by itself, evidence that two distinct signals become indistinguishable to the model. Structural aliasing is therefore treated as an emergent, falsifiable property of the trained representations\cite{paganiPreliminaryInsightsChronos2026}. At a phase-lock consecutive patch tokens coincide, $\mathbf{t}_k=\mathbf{t}_{k+1}$, so distinct inputs can share a token sequence. We diagnose the phenomenon by \emph{readability} rather than by a fixed distance between representations: structural aliasing is present when the signal's frequency can no longer be recovered from the model's internal state at a locked frequency as well as it can at neighbouring, non-locked ones.

\subsection{Analysis of Structural Degeneracies and Invariances}

\autoref{eq:flock_set} is the union of two distinct families,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branches}
\mathcal{F}_{S}
=
\left\{
c\frac{f_s}{S}
\right\}_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{+}},
\qquad
\mathcal{F}_{P}
=
\left\{
k\frac{f_s}{P}
\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}},
\qquad
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lock}}
=
\left(\mathcal{F}_{S}\cup\mathcal{F}_{P}\right)
\cap
\left[0,\tfrac{f_s}{2}\right),
\end{equation}

which coincide if and only if $S=P$. The two families act on the token sequence in structurally different ways, and it is this difference, rather than the locking condition alone, that determines the behaviour of $\mathcal{T}_{P,S}$.

\subsubsection{Stride branch: inter-patch degeneracy}
For $f\in\mathcal{F}_{S}$ the signal satisfies \autoref{eq:general_periodicity}, and \autoref{eq:general_token_collapse} applies directly: every token equals its predecessor, so the token sequence collapses into a static series of identical vectors. Two signals that agree on a single window of length $P$ and share the stride period are then mapped to the same sequence, so $\mathcal{T}_{P,S}$ is not injective on this class. The stride branch therefore produces a genuine \emph{degeneracy}: distinct inputs collide, and all inter-token temporal variation is eliminated.

\subsubsection{Patch branch: token-space invariance}
For $f\in\mathcal{F}_{P}$ the signal period $T_0=f_s/f$ divides $P$, so $x$ is $P$-periodic and every token spans an integer number of complete cycles. Using \autoref{eq:element_def},

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:circular_shift}
\mathbf{t}_k[m]
=
x[kS+m]
=
x\!\left[(kS+m)\bmod P\right]
=
\mathbf{t}_0\!\left[(kS+m)\bmod P\right],
\end{equation}

so that

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:token_orbit}
\mathbf{t}_k
=
\mathbf{C}^{\,kS \bmod P}\,\mathbf{t}_0,
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{C}$ denotes the circular left-shift operator on $\mathbb{R}^{P}$, $(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v})[m]=\mathbf{v}[(m+1)\bmod P]$. Every token is a circular shift of the first, and the token alphabet is finite, containing at most $P/\gcd(P,S)$ distinct elements irrespective of the length of the context window. This is an \emph{invariance} rather than a collapse: consecutive tokens need not be identical, but the whole sequence is generated by a single vector under a group action, and carries no information about the signal beyond $\mathbf{t}_0$ and the token index.

\subsubsection{Case 1: Equal Patch Size and Stride ($P=S$)}
When patches are contiguous and non-overlapping the two branches coincide, $\mathcal{F}_{S}=\mathcal{F}_{P}$, and $kS \bmod P = 0$ for every $k$. The circular shift of \autoref{eq:token_orbit} reduces to the identity, and the invariance of the patch branch degenerates into the collapse of the stride branch,

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{t}_k
=
\mathbf{t}_0,
\qquad
\forall k.
\end{equation}

This is the worst case of the family: it is the only geometry in which \emph{every} locked frequency produces exact and total collapse, with no residual structure in the token sequence. It is also the default configuration of the target architecture, which operates at $P=S=16$.

\subsubsection{Case 2: Overlapping Windows ($S<P$)}
When the stride is smaller than the patch length the two branches separate. Since $f_s/S>f_s/P$, the stride comb is the sparser of the two: strictly below Nyquist it contributes $\lceil S/2 \rceil - 1$ members against the $\lceil P/2 \rceil - 1$ of the patch comb, and whenever $S$ divides $P$ the stride comb is a subset of the patch comb. Most locked frequencies in this regime therefore belong to $\mathcal{F}_{P}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{S}$ and produce the shift-invariance of \autoref{eq:token_orbit}, not total collapse.

Overlap does not weaken the exact condition. The derivation of \autoref{eq:general_token_collapse} makes no use of the relation between $P$ and $S$, so at $f\in\mathcal{F}_{S}$ consecutive tokens are still exactly equal. What overlap changes is the behaviour \emph{around} a lock: adjacent windows share $P-S$ samples by construction, so two consecutive tokens are constrained to agree on their common support regardless of frequency, and the transition into collapse is correspondingly smoother. Reducing the stride at fixed patch size therefore raises the lowest member of $\mathcal{F}_{S}$, thins the stride comb below Nyquist, and softens the local shape of the effect without removing it.

\subsubsection{Case 3: Disjoint Windows ($S>P$)}
When the stride exceeds the patch length the windows leave $S-P$ unobserved samples between consecutive patches: the operator observes only the intervals $[kS,\,kS+P-1]$, while the regions $[kS+P,\,(k+1)S-1]$ are never seen. Two signals that coincide on every observed window but differ on the gaps are mapped to the same token sequence, so $\mathcal{T}_{P,S}$ is non-injective for \emph{any} $f$, independently of the locking condition. The resulting loss of information cannot be attributed to the interaction between signal periodicity and the tokenization grid, since it is already implied by the discarded samples. Configurations with $S>P$ are therefore excluded by construction from the experimental design, so that any observed degradation remains attributable to tokenization geometry rather than to missing data.

\subsubsection{Consequences for observability}
Across all three cases the effect on the token sequence is the same in kind: at $f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lock}}$ the operator $\mathcal{T}_{P,S}$ ceases to be injective, and the frequency $f$ can no longer be identified from the tokens alone. Under the stride branch the sequence carries a single vector; under the patch branch it carries a single vector and an index. In both cases the quantity that the downstream model would need in order to recover $f$ has been removed before the first attention layer, and no property of the trained weights can restore it. Whether that formal loss actually translates into a measurable loss of \emph{readability} in the learned representations is an empirical question, and it is the object of the hypotheses formulated in \autoref{sec:four}.
